Guide to Importing Indian Cocopeat Blocks: EC, Expansion & Quality Testing Standards

By Indus Roots Technical Agronomy Desk • Origin: Pollachi, Tamil Nadu, India • 5 Min Read

For commercial greenhouse operators, commercial berry growers, and horticultural potting soil blenders across the globe, Indian cocopeat (coir pith) has become the gold standard soilless growing medium. Sourced primarily from Tamil Nadu’s coconut heartland around Pollachi and Coimbatore, compressed 5kg cocopeat blocks offer exceptional water-holding capacity, optimal air-filled porosity (AFP), and eco-friendly circularity.

However, international procurement heads often face significant quality variations when importing from unverified brokers. Below is a comprehensive technical breakdown of the key parameters you must specify and verify when importing Indian cocopeat blocks.

1. Understanding Electrical Conductivity (EC): Low EC vs. High EC

Electrical Conductivity (EC) measures the concentration of soluble salts (primarily sodium, potassium, and chloride ions) present in the coir pith. Because coconut trees thrive near coastal and tropical zones, raw untreated coir has naturally high salinity.

  • Washed Low EC Grade (< 0.5 mS/cm): Essential for sensitive horticultural crops, greenhouse hydroponics (tomatoes, strawberries, capsicum), and seedlings. The raw coir is repeatedly washed with sweet groundwater on concrete drying floors until the leachate reads below 0.5 mS/cm.
  • Unwashed High EC Grade (> 1.5 mS/cm): Economical grade suitable for mature landscaping, soil conditioning in arid regions, golf courses, or animal/equine bedding where root salt sensitivity is not a constraint.

2. Testing Methodology: 1:1.5 vs. 1:5 Volume Dilution

A common point of misunderstanding between international buyers and Indian suppliers is the dilution method used during laboratory EC testing:

The 1:1.5 Dilution Method (European / Dutch Standard):

One volume of reconstituted cocopeat is mixed with 1.5 volumes of demineralized water. This standard closely replicates the real-world rootzone rhizosphere. At Indus Roots Exports, our Low EC blocks test consistently at < 0.5 mS/cm under 1:1.5.

3. Expansion Volume & Yield Benchmarks

High-grade 5kg blocks are compressed at an approximate 5:1 ratio. Quality blocks must expand cleanly and rapidly upon the addition of water (approx. 20-25 liters of water per 5kg block).

A standard export-grade block from Indus Roots yields 15 to 16 liters of expanded media per kilogram, resulting in 75+ liters of fluffy, aerated substrate per 5kg block. If a supplier's block yields under 65 liters, it often indicates excessive compaction pressure that crushed the cellular structure, or high sand content.

4. Container Loadability for 40ft High Cube (HC) Containers

Ocean freight represents a significant percentage of landed CIF cost. Maximizing container payload is paramount:

  • Palletized Cargo: 24 wooden or plastic pallets per 40ft HC. Each pallet holds approx. 210 to 220 blocks (~5,200 blocks total). Total payload: approx. 24 to 26 Metric Tons.
  • Floor Loaded (Unpalletized): Blocks loaded loose into container floor. Allows up to 26 - 27 Metric Tons, but requires significant manual labor for destination unloading.

5. Mandatory Export Clearances: Phytosanitary & Fumigation

Before arriving at ports such as Jebel Ali, Rotterdam, Felixstowe, or New York, Indian cocopeat shipments must be accompanied by:

  • Phytosanitary Certificate: Issued by the Directorate of Plant Protection, Quarantine & Storage, Government of India, verifying freedom from weed seeds, insects, and soil pests.
  • Fumigation Certificate: Treatment with Methyl Bromide (MBr) or Phosphine according to destination port quarantine biosecurity regulations.
  • ISPM-15 Heat Treated Pallets: Ensure pallets carry official IPPC stamps to prevent rejection at destination customs.
